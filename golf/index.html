My Golf Journey

Legacy

It’s a word that has followed me since the day I stepped onto campus as an eager freshman.

I came to Saint Mary’s with big dreams. I wanted to break records, win championships, and help take our team to new heights. I wanted to do it all and win it all.

But more than anything, I wanted to leave a legacy. My dad and I talked about it the first time we walked campus: a legacy of leadership, hard work, impact, and leaving things better than I found them.

Where it started

When I first picked up a golf club at seven years old during a summer camp, I never could have imagined where this game would take me. It turned into nearly two decades of lessons, challenges, friendships, victories, setbacks, growth, and memories that have shaped who I am today.

...

Divine Child

At Dearborn Divine Child, golf turned into something bigger than me. Our team set a goal as freshmen to win the program’s first state championship, and we spent four years chasing it, climbing the standings each season, even through a move up to Division 2.

In October 2021, my senior year, we finally did it. We trailed by six strokes after the first day at Bedford Valley in Battle Creek, then came back to win the MHSAA Lower Peninsula Division 2 title by nine. I cut five strokes off my first round to finish seventh individually. Finishing the thing we’d set out to do as freshmen, together, is still one of my favorite moments in the game.

...

Saint Mary’s College

I took my game to Saint Mary’s College in Notre Dame, Indiana, where I competed for four years as a Belle in NCAA Division III while studying Computing & Applied Mathematics. College golf is a different kind of commitment: early lifts, practice between classes, long van rides, and tournaments that can take you out of class for days at a time.

Our team won three straight MIAA conference championships (2024, 2025, 2026) and earned a trip to the NCAA Division III Championship each of those years. As a sophomore at the 2024 NCAA Championship, I shot a one-under 71 in a round that was suspended twice by weather. It was the lowest 18-hole score by a Saint Mary’s golfer in NCAA Championship history.

...

“Character is built in the moments nobody sees.” Julia Lizak

The last round

My senior season was one I’ll always be proud of. I finished runner-up at the Trine Spring Classic, which our team won, and then runner-up again at the MIAA Championship, going 75-72, as we claimed our third straight conference title. We ended it all at the NCAA Championship at Desert Willow in Palm Desert, California.

The scores in that last tournament weren’t what I wanted. But walking off the final green, I wasn’t thinking about the scorecard. I was thinking about everything golf had given me over the years.

Leadership

Wearing the “C”

I was a captain at Divine Child and a two-time captain at Saint Mary’s. Those roles taught me more about leadership than any class could.

Golf is an individual sport played as a team. Everyone has their own round and their own scorecard, but only the team score goes up on the board. As a captain, my job was to hold both of those at once: help each teammate get the most out of their own game, while keeping all of us pulling in the same direction.

A lot of that work happens where no one sees it. It looks like checking in with a teammate after a rough round, helping freshmen figure out how to balance classes with a travel schedule, setting the tone at an early practice, and being the same person whether I shot 72 or 82.

...

What I learned about leading

I learned that leadership isn’t about being the loudest voice in the room. It’s about serving others, setting the example, lifting up your teammates, and putting the team before yourself.

Beyond the Course

How golf shows up in my career

I’m not playing competitively anymore, but I bring the game with me into every internship, project, and classroom.

  1. Staying steady under pressure

    A four-foot putt to close out a round teaches you how to stay calm when it counts. I lean on that same composure in a presentation, at a deadline, or when something breaks.

  2. Owning the outcome

    In golf there’s no one else to blame. You post your number and you learn from it. I approach my work the same way: take ownership, figure out what went wrong, and get better.

  3. Process over results

    Good scores come from good habits. The daily reps, the routine, and the prep add up. That mindset is how I approach learning new tools and building projects.

  4. Managing my time

    Balancing NCAA athletics with a Computing & Applied Mathematics degree, and now a master’s at Notre Dame, taught me to plan ahead and protect my time.

  5. Building relationships

    So much business happens on a golf course. From an NCR intern outing to a round with colleagues, the game opens doors and starts conversations.

Looking Back

Thank you, golf

Golf has given me so much more than a sport. It taught me accountability, resilience, discipline, humility, and how to lead.

It taught me how to set goals, hold myself to a high standard, and keep showing up even when the results weren’t immediate. It taught me that character is built in the moments nobody sees.

To my coaches, teammates, and everyone who has been part of this journey: thank you. Thank you for challenging me, believing in me, helping me become the person I am today, and for all of the memories I will cherish forever.

And most importantly, Mom and Dad, thank you. Thank you for the countless hours, miles traveled, weekends spent on golf courses, sacrifices made, every encouraging word and phone call, and your unwavering belief in me. There is no way to put into words how grateful I am.

No matter how legacy may be found, I hope I left Saint Mary’s a little better than I found it. The lessons, relationships, and values this game gave me will stay with me forever.

I still strive for more. Still eager to learn. Still determined to lead. Still committed to building a legacy wherever life takes me next.

Thank you, competitive golf, for everything.

Go Belles forever.
